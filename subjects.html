Six subjects, six working scales

Each area lists its core topics and links through to the matching notes. Start wherever your coursework does.

0.01–50 Hz

Seismology

The study of elastic waves through the Earth — how earthquakes and controlled sources generate them, how layering bends and reflects them, and what the resulting records reveal about structure and source.

  • P-waves, S-waves, and surface waves
  • Travel-time curves and the Wadati diagram
  • Seismic reflection and refraction
  • Earthquake magnitude and moment
  • Focal mechanisms and beachball diagrams
  • Seismic hazard and site response

µGal precision

Gravity & Geodesy

Small variations in gravitational acceleration map density differences below the surface — and the shape of the Earth itself. This covers the corrections needed to isolate a real anomaly from noise.

  • Free-air and Bouguer corrections
  • Isostasy and Airy vs. Pratt models
  • The geoid and reference ellipsoid
  • Gravimeter drift and tidal corrections
  • Terrain corrections
  • Satellite gravimetry (GRACE, GOCE)

nT · IGRF

Geomagnetism

The Earth's magnetic field, generated by the geodynamo, drifts and reverses over geological time — and its frozen record in rock is one of the strongest lines of evidence for plate tectonics.

  • The main field and the IGRF model
  • Secular variation and the geodynamo
  • Paleomagnetism and apparent polar wander
  • Magnetic surveying and anomaly maps
  • Curie depth and thermal structure
  • Magnetic reversals and seafloor striping

Ma timescale

Geodynamics & Tectonics

Plate motion, mantle flow, and the forces that build mountains and open oceans — the large-scale processes that everything else in geophysics is ultimately explaining.

  • Divergent, convergent, and transform margins
  • Mantle convection and plume theory
  • Stress, strain, and rheology of rock
  • Subduction zone dynamics
  • Isostatic rebound
  • Plate reconstruction methods

0–5 km depth

Exploration Geophysics

Applied methods for imaging the shallow subsurface — for hydrocarbons, minerals, groundwater, and site investigation — where survey design and processing choices directly shape what you can resolve.

  • Reflection survey design and stacking
  • Refraction and near-surface velocity models
  • Well logging and log correlation
  • Electrical and electromagnetic methods
  • Ground-penetrating radar
  • Migration and depth conversion

10⁻¹–10³ km scale

Remote Sensing

Satellite and airborne platforms extend geophysical measurement across scales that ground surveys can't reach, from centimeter-scale deformation to continent-wide gravity fields.

  • InSAR and surface deformation
  • Satellite gravimetry and altimetry
  • Multispectral and thermal imaging
  • LiDAR and digital elevation models
  • GNSS geodesy
  • Change detection over time series
